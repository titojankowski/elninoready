# EL NIÑO READY
## A disaster you can see coming is a disaster you can prevent.

**The biggest El Niño in history is forming right now.**

NOAA puts the odds at 75% that this becomes the strongest El Niño ever measured. It peaks in December, about three months from today. Hundreds of millions of people will feel it in their power, their water, and their livelihoods.

> "The models are forecasting something outside the envelope of anything we have ever observed."
> Zeke Hausfather, climate scientist, Berkeley Earth

### What the forecasts say

- The Pacific is forecast to hit +4.0 °C or more, nearly a full degree beyond anything ever measured. 91 of every 100 model runs break the all-time record.
- 49 million more people won't have enough to eat by the end of next year, joining 225 million already going hungry. Central America is hit hardest.
- Indonesia is already burning, with more fires this year than any year on record. The last time a super El Niño dried out the peat, an estimated 100,000 people died from the smoke.
- Epidemics follow the shifted rains. Malaria, dengue, and cholera have come after every major El Niño.
- 2027 is forecast to be the hottest year humans have ever lived through.
- $17.6 trillion is the projected cost to the world over the five years that follow. Most of it is still preventable.

### Why this is different from most climate news

These deaths are the preventable kind. Smoke, hunger, disease, and heat kill on a delay, weeks to months after a forecast that already exists. People die when the warning doesn't reach them, when the money arrives after the disaster instead of before, and when the plan stays in the drawer.

Three things save lives, and all three reward acting before the peak:

1. **Warnings reach people.** In time, in their language, with something to do attached.
2. **Money moves early.** A dollar of food, water treatment, or backup power staged before the disaster beats a dollar of relief after it.
3. **Systems hold.** Grids hardened for heat, water stored where drought and flood risk is rising, contingency plans rehearsed instead of filed.
