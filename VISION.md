# El Niño Ready

**The mission: save lives during the strongest El Niño ever measured.**

El Niño Ready is a volunteer effort with one idea at its core: the 2026–27 El Niño is forecast, and forecast disasters are preventable ones. Its first campaign is **El Niño Climate Week**, September 20–27, 2026, run parallel to New York Climate Week.
[elninoclimateweek.com](https://elninoclimateweek.com)

---

## The big idea

The strongest El Niño ever measured is forecast to peak in December. NOAA puts the odds at 69% that it breaks every record. Economists project a central cost of **$17.6 trillion** over the five years that follow.

But the number that matters most isn't in dollars. When a super El Niño last dried out Indonesia's peat, an estimated **100,000 people died from the smoke**. This one is forecast to push **49 million more people into hunger**. Epidemics — malaria, dengue, cholera — have followed the shifted rains of every major El Niño. Then comes the heat: 2027 is forecast to be the hottest year humans have ever lived through.

Here's the thing about those deaths: they are the preventable kind. Smoke, hunger, disease, and heat kill on a delay — weeks to months after a forecast that already exists. People die when the warning doesn't reach them, when the money arrives after the disaster instead of before, when the plan stays in the drawer.

**What success looks like: when this El Niño is over, lives were saved because people were ready.** We'll never count them precisely — saved lives don't leave records. But that's the bar, and every action below points at it.

## How lives get saved

Three levers, all of which reward action *before* the peak:

- **Warnings reach people.** Forecasts only save lives if they arrive in time, in the right language, with something actionable attached. Expanding who's talking about El Niño — from climate conferences to run clubs to group chats in affected regions — is literally building this system.
- **Money moves early.** A dollar of anticipatory action — pre-positioned food, water treatment, backup power — outperforms a dollar of post-disaster relief. The window to move it is now.
- **Systems hold.** Grids hardened for heat and storms, water storage pre-positioned where drought and flood risk is elevated, contingency plans taken out of the drawer and rehearsed.

## The first campaign: El Niño Climate Week

**September 20–27, 2026 · New York + everywhere**

Every September, the climate community gathers in New York — the world's largest climate gathering, happening three months before the peak. Breakfasts, run clubs, networking dinners. To the people who will actually live through this El Niño, that can look like climate isn't all that urgent.

El Niño Climate Week points that gathering at the storm. It's how El Niño Ready recruits: every event is a room where a warning spreads, money gets moved, or a plan gets picked up.

**Success on September 27:**

1. **Events carried it.** 20+ events during Climate Week — in New York and online — put El Niño on their agenda, in their theme, or on their stage. An event "counts" when it's on the public calendar at elninoclimateweek.com, so at any moment anyone can see the number. This is the campaign's scoreboard.
2. **A readiness coalition exists.** The week ends with people organized around the three-month window before the peak — working the three levers above.
3. **The narrative shifted.** El Niño became a thing everyone at Climate Week was talking about — on stages, in hallways, in the press covering the week.

## Five ways to get involved

### 1. Find the events already happening

Climate Week NYC lists hundreds of events. Some already touch El Niño — extreme heat, food security, insurance, adaptation — and don't know they're part of this. We read the Climate Week NYC program and announcements, find every event with an El Niño connection, and put them on our website as the first public El Niño Climate Week calendar.

*Good for: anyone with a laptop and an hour.*

### 2. Add El Niño to an event that already exists

The fastest way to create an El Niño event is to borrow one. If you're running an event — or know someone who is — the ask is small: add El Niño to your theme, your opening remarks, one panel question, one discussion topic.

Can we get a run club to run for El Niño? A dinner where the table conversation is the three-month window? A breakfast that opens with the forecast? Yes — that's the whole idea.

*Good for: event organizers, and anyone who can make an intro to one.*

### 3. Create a new event

Some events should exist that don't yet: a forecast briefing, a readiness workshop, a solutions session on power, water, or getting warnings to people. Online events count fully — this El Niño is global, and so is the week.

You don't need to bring a complete event. Bring a piece: an idea, a speaker, a venue, a contact, an audience. We'll help assemble the pieces into events.

*Good for: people with an idea, a venue, a network, or a topic they can teach.*

### 4. Volunteer an hour (or four)

Not everyone wants to run an event. Some people just want a task. We keep a live task list — research an event, draft an outreach email, update the website, reach out to a venue. Tell us how much time you've got, take a task, and run with it.

*Good for: anyone. This is the "I want to help but don't know how" door.*

### 5. Amplify

The cheapest yes: share it. Post the forecast. Put El Niño Climate Week in your newsletter. Introduce us to a journalist, a podcast, a community that should know. Every share is the first lever — a warning reaching someone it hadn't reached before.

*Good for: everyone, in under two minutes.*
